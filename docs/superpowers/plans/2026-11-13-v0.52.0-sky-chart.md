# v0.52.0 — Sky chart: implementation plan

> **For agentic workers:** Codex implements one phase at a time under
> `docs/agent-collaboration.md` §4; Claude reviews each phase. Steps use
> checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax. Never stage, commit or push.

**Goal:** a solved project's Info tab shows a compact star chart of the
picture's constellation, the date the picture's centre is on the meridian at
midnight, and an "Open the sky chart" button. The button opens a full-screen
atlas you can pan and zoom, from the whole sky down to your own picture
drawn in place.

**Architecture:**
- **Backend:** the Info endpoint's `SolveField` gains the solve ID, a
  plate solve's stored WCS, and the midnight-meridian date.
  - The date comes from a pure `midnight_meridian_date` in
    `services/astronomy.py`.
- **Sky data:** a generator script turns d3-celestial 0.7.35's data files
  into one compact `frontend/src/data/sky/sky.json`, committed with
  d3-celestial's licence.
- **Frontend:**
  - pure view geometry and drawing rules in `lib/skyChart/`;
  - one canvas renderer;
  - a compact chart and a full-screen dialog, both lazily loaded with the
    data.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.14, FastAPI, Pydantic, astropy; React, TypeScript,
MUI, TanStack Query, d3 (already a dependency). No new dependency, no
migration.

**Spec:** [sky chart design](../specs/2026-11-13-v0.52.0-sky-chart-design.md),
approved by Fred on 2026-11-13. Mockup (local, ignored):
`.superpowers/mockups/v0.52.0/sky-chart.html`, served at
`http://127.0.0.1:5191/sky-chart.html`, Chart B. Where they differ, the

## Global constraints

- **Coordinates:** RA in degrees from 0 (inclusive) to 360 (exclusive),
  J2000, everywhere outside the generator's input. The source files store RA
  as a longitude from −180° to 180°; convert with `ra % 360`.
- **Projection:**
  `d3.geoStereographic().rotate([-ra, -dec]).reflectX(true).clipAngle(150)`.
  - Scale for a span *S* across the shorter side *m*:
    `m / (2 * tan(S/4))`, with angles in radians.
  - Pixels per degree at the centre: `scale * π / 360`.
- **Spans:**
  - *S* from 0.1° to 180°;
  - the constellation view is clamped to 20°–120°, with an 8% margin on
    each side;
  - "Your picture" is 3.5 × the picture's longer side;
  - "Whole sky" is 180°.
- **Drawing rules** (spec §2):
  - **Stars:** radius `max(0.55, (6.4 − mag) × 0.62 × k)` px, with
    `k = clamp(84 / S, 0.5, 1.25)`. The magnitude limit is 4.5 when S ≥ 150,
    5.2 when S > 80, and none otherwise.
  - **Grid:** `[15, 10]` when S > 80, `[5, 5]` when S > 20, `[1, 1]` when
    S > 6, otherwise `[0.25, 0.25]`.
  - **Stop short:** trim each end by the star's radius + 3 px, at most 40%
    of the segment's length.
  - **Names:** all of them when S < 150; otherwise rank 1 plus the picture's
    constellation.
  - **The picture:**
    - a reticle when its outline's longer edge is under 10 px;
    - the outline from 10 px;
    - the image from 46 px, drawn as an adaptive mesh (spec §4):
      - start from 8 × 8 cells, and skip off-screen cells and cells with a
        corner more than 120° from the centre;
      - split a visible cell when its exact projected centre is more than
        0.25 CSS px from the two triangles' estimate, up to depth 10 and
        4,096 triangles per frame;
      - clip each triangle to its edges offset outward by 0.6 px (miter
        capped at 3 px), and draw only the inverse-mapped source rectangle
        plus 2 image px.
  - **Scale bar:** the largest of 60°, 30°, 20°, 10°, 5°, 2°, 1°, 30′, 10′,
    5′ and 2′ that fits in 110 px, or 2′ when none fits.
- **Exact copy:**
  - `Open the sky chart`;
  - `On the meridian at midnight around {Month D}`, with the date in
    semibold, as in `April 23`;
  - `The sky chart couldn't load. Reload the page to try again.`;
  - the header subtitle:
    - `{A} in {Con}`;
    - `{A} and {B} in {Con}`;
    - `{n} targets in {Con}`;
    - `Centred in {Con}` when there are no targets.

    Each target is named with `targetLabel()` from `lib/objectFacts.ts`,
    and `{Con}` comes from `displayConstellation()`;
  - buttons `Your picture`, `Constellation`, `Whole sky`, `+` (aria-label
    `Zoom in`) and `−` (aria-label `Zoom out`), and the close button's
    aria-label `Close the sky chart`;
  - legend entries `Your picture's outline (its real size and rotation)`,
    `Constellation lines` and `{Con} boundary`;
  - the canvas's aria-label `Sky chart showing {project} in {Con}`;
  - its hidden description `Drag or use the arrow keys to move around.
    Scroll, pinch, or press + and − to zoom. Press Home to return to the
    constellation.`;
  - the picture's label: the project name, shortened to its first 31
    characters plus `…` when it is over 32.
- **Colours:** the spec §7 table, exactly, in one module. Night is used in
  the dark theme and Paper in the light. Six-digit hex only.
- **Tablet rules** (development decisions, "Tablet and LAN behavior"):
  - `touch-action: none` on the canvas;
  - touch listeners `{ passive: false }`;
  - suppress `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` on the
    container;
  - `user-select: none` and `-webkit-touch-callout: none`.
- **No new dependencies.** Import d3 as `import * as d3 from "d3"`, as other
  charts do.

  ```json

                         "constellations.bounds.json": "…", "constellations.json": "…"}},

    "figures": {"UMa": [[i, j, k, …], …], …},

    "labels": {"UMa": {"rank": 1, "points": [{"at": [ra, dec]}]},

                                             {"at": [ra, dec], "name": "Serpens Cauda"}]}, …}
  }
  ```

    - positions to 4 decimals, magnitudes to 2;
    - sorted by magnitude, then RA, then Dec;
    - `EXTRA_STARS` added before sorting.
  - **Figures:**
    - each source polyline becomes star indices;
    - each vertex snaps to the nearest star within 0.01°, by great-circle
      distance;
    - if none is that close, `SkyDataError` names the constellation and the
      vertex.
  - **Boundaries:**
    - the source's sparse vertices, 3 decimals, rings left as given;
    - the frontend fills in points (C1 `densifyRing`);
    - both Serpens features go under `Ser`.
  - **Labels:**
    - `rank` as an int;
    - positions to 2 decimals;
    - a `name` only when two features share a code (Serpens), from each
      feature's `properties.name`.
  - **Source:** the SHA-256 of the four input files, and `version` from the
    package's `package.json`.

## Phase C — frontend (Codex)

### Task C1: View geometry and drawing rules (pure)

**Files:**
- Create:
  - `frontend/src/lib/skyChart/geometry.ts`;
  - `frontend/src/lib/skyChart/text.ts`;
  - `frontend/src/lib/skyChart/palette.ts`;
  - `frontend/src/lib/skyChart/data.ts`;
  - `frontend/scripts/check-sky-chart.mjs`, the committed assertion
    script.
- Modify: `frontend/package.json`, adding the script
  `"check:sky": "node scripts/check-sky-chart.mjs"` (no dependency).

**Interfaces:**
- **Produces, in `geometry.ts`:**
  - `type SkyCoord = [ra: number, dec: number]`.
  - `interface SkyView { centre: SkyCoord; spanDeg: number }`.
  - Constants `MIN_SPAN_DEG = 0.1`, `MAX_SPAN_DEG = 180`,
    `CLIP_DEG = 150`, `WHOLE_SKY_SPAN_DEG = 180`.
  - Projection and view functions:
    - `scaleForSpan(spanDeg: number, shortSidePx: number): number`;
    - `makeProjection(view: SkyView, widthPx: number, heightPx: number): d3.GeoProjection`;
    - `pxPerDegree(view: SkyView, widthPx: number, heightPx: number): number`;
    - `anchorView(view: SkyView, spanDeg: number, grabbed: SkyCoord, pointerPx: [number, number], widthPx, heightPx): { view: SkyView; residualPx: number }`:
      the shared solver for panning and zooming.
      - It finds the centre (at span `spanDeg`) whose projection puts the
        sky point `grabbed` under `pointerPx`.
      - It re-centres iteratively: at most 12 passes, stopping early once
        within 0.25 px, or when a pass no longer improves the residual by
        1%.
      - It returns the best view found and its residual, with the centre's
        Dec clamped to [−90, 90].
      - It never returns NaN: a non-finite step keeps the previous best.
      - Some anchors can't be reached with north up, near a pole. Then it
        returns the best reachable view, and its residual says so.

      (Codex's plan review: the iteration count is a limit, not a proof of
      convergence; a fixed count can't promise 1 px.)
    - `panView(view, grabbed: SkyCoord, pointerPx: [number, number], widthPx, heightPx): SkyView`:
      `anchorView` at the current span. The grabbed point is taken at
      pointer-down by inverting the projection, so the sky follows the
      pointer. (Codex's plan review showed a displacement-only pan slips
      6 px at S=60 and 93 px at S=180 when grabbed off-centre.)
    - `panViewBy(view, dxPx, dyPx, widthPx, heightPx): SkyView`, for the
      keys: `panView` with the sky point at the canvas centre as `grabbed`,
      and the centre plus (dx, dy) as the pointer;
    - `zoomViewAt(view, factor, pointPx: [number, number], widthPx, heightPx): SkyView`:
      - `factor` > 1 zooms in, and the span is clamped to [MIN, MAX];
      - it calls `anchorView` with the sky point under `pointPx` before the
        zoom, so that point stays under the pointer;
      - the pole exception is the same as for panning;
    - `fitConstellationView(rings: SkyCoord[][], pictureCentre: SkyCoord, widthPx, heightPx): SkyView`:
      fits the projected bounding box of every ring vertex plus the
      picture's centre, centred, with an 8% margin on each side, clamped to
      20°–120°;
    - `tanPixelToSky(wcs: SolveWcs, x: number, y: number): SkyCoord`: the
      same formula as `services/object_search.py:_tan_pixel_to_sky`, with
      zero-based pixel centres (`dx = x + 1 − crpix1`), RA in [0, 360);
    - `pictureOutline(wcs: SolveWcs, perEdge = 8): SkyCoord[]`: clockwise
      from the top-left corner along the raster edges, with `x0 = y0 =
      −0.5`, `x1 = width − 0.5` and `y1 = height − 0.5`. It has
      `4 × perEdge` points, and the corners are at indices 0, n, 2n and 3n;
    - `pictureLongerSideDeg(wcs: SolveWcs): number`: the larger
      great-circle length of the top or the left edge;
    - `pictureView(centre: SkyCoord, longerSideDeg: number): SkyView`:
      3.5 ×, clamped;
    - `wholeSkyView(centre: SkyCoord): SkyView`;
    - `interpolateView(a: SkyView, b: SkyView): (t: number) => SkyView`:
      `d3.geoInterpolate` for the centre, logarithmic for the span;
    - `densifyRing(ring: SkyCoord[], maxStepDeg = 0.25): SkyCoord[]`:
      - interpolates linearly in RA, the shorter way round, and in Dec, so
        no step exceeds `maxStepDeg` in either;
      - keeps every input vertex, returns a closed ring, and keeps RA in
        [0, 360);
      - the renderer caches the result for each code.
  - Picture-placement functions (the mesh):
    - `triangleAffine(src: Tri, dst: Tri): [a, b, c, d, e, f]`, where
      `type Tri = [[number, number], [number, number], [number, number]]`:
      the `setTransform` matrix mapping each image-pixel vertex of `src`
      onto its CSS-px vertex in `dst`;
    - `growTriangle(tri: Tri, px: number, miterCapPx = 3): Tri`: each edge
      is offset outward by `px` along its normal, and the new vertices are
      where the offset edges meet. A vertex's move is capped at
      `miterCapPx`;
    - `sourceRect(grownDst: Tri, affine, imageW: number, imageH: number, marginPx = 2): { sx: number; sy: number; sw: number; sh: number } | null`:
      - the bounding box, in image pixels, of the grown destination
        triangle mapped back through the inverse of `affine`;
      - widened by `marginPx`, clamped to the image, and floored or
        ceiled to whole pixels;
      - `null` when empty;
    - `adaptiveMesh(opts: { wcs: SolveWcs; imageW: number; imageH: number; project: (c: SkyCoord) => [number, number] | null; isFar: (c: SkyCoord) => boolean; viewport: { width: number; height: number }; tolerancePx?: number; maxDepth?: number; maxTriangles?: number }): { src: Tri; dst: Tri }[]`:
      - the defaults are 0.25, 10 and 4,096;
      - image pixel `u = (x + 0.5) × imageW / wcs.width`, and likewise
        `v`;
      - it starts from 8 × 8 cells over the raster;
      - it skips a cell when any corner `isFar` (more than 120° from the

**Interfaces:**

- `PINNED_INPUTS: dict[str, str]`, the five files in the table above.
- **`read_hyg_stars(path: Path) -> list[HygStar]`**, where `HygStar` is a
  frozen dataclass (`ra`, `dec`, `mag`, `hip: int | None`). It reads the
  gzipped CSV with `csv.DictReader`:
  - `ra = round((float(ra_hours) * 15) % 360, 4)`, with 360 wrapped to 0;
  - `dec` rounded to 4 decimals, `mag` to 2;
  - it skips `id == "0"` and rows with an empty `mag`.
- **`select_stars(rows, figure_vertices) -> tuple[list[list[float]], list[int | None]]`.**
  - Keep the rows with `mag <= 6.0`.
  - Rows at an identical rounded `(ra, dec)` merge into the brightest; on a
    tie, keep the lower HIP.
  - Then, for each figure vertex with no kept star within
    `SNAP_DISTANCE_DEG`, add the nearest row of any magnitude within that
    distance. Only Mira (HIP 10826, 6.47) is added this way.
  - Sort by `(mag, ra, dec)`. Return `[ra, dec, mag]` lists and the
    parallel HIP list.
  - Figure snapping then runs against this list, as now.
- **`parse_iau_star_names(text: str) -> dict[int, str]`.** Map each HIP
  number to its `Name/Diacritics` value.
  - The name is characters 18–35 of the line, stripped; the columns align
    by characters.
  - Read the HIP number from the tail pattern
    `(\S+)\s+(\S+)\s+(\S+)\s+(\S+)\s+(\S+)\s+(\S+)\s+(\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2})\s*\*?\s*$`
    (group 3; `_` means none).
  - Skip blank lines and lines starting with `#` or `$`.
  - Raise `SkyDataError` for an unmatched data line, a repeated HIP number,
    or no names.
  - The real file has 451 data rows, 411 of them with a HIP number.
- **`read_stellarium_edges(index_json: dict) -> list[Edge]`.** `Edge` holds
  the two vertex IDs, the kind (`"M"` or `"P"`), the two B1875 corners in
  degrees, and the two constellation codes. Raise `SkyDataError` for any
  kind other than `M+`/`P+`, a malformed line, or `edges_epoch` other than
  `"B1875"`.
- **`build_rings(edges, codes: Iterable[str]) -> dict[str, list[list[Edge-oriented]]]`.**
  - Map `SER1`/`SER2` to `Ser`, and other codes to d3-celestial's spelling,
    ignoring case.
  - For each constellation, chain its edges by vertex ID into closed loops,
    orienting each edge to follow the chain.
  - Raise `SkyDataError` for an unknown code, a ring that doesn't close, or
    a vertex with other than two of the constellation's edges.
  - Serpens gets two rings; every other constellation gets one.
- **`edge_at(edge, t: float) -> tuple[float, float]`:** the B1875 point
  at parameter t in [0, 1] along the edge, always in the edge's own
  direction. `M`: Dec varies at constant RA. `P`: RA runs east from ra1 to
  ra2, wrapping through 360°, at constant Dec.
- **`precess_b1875_to_j2000(points) -> list[tuple[float, float]]`:**
  astropy, from `FK4(equinox=Time("B1875"))` to `FK5(equinox="J2000")`,
  vectorised.
- **`linear_fill(a, b, t) -> tuple[float, float]`:** the frontend's
  interpolation between two J2000 points: linear in RA and Dec, with RA
  taken the short way round. Port the rule from `densifyRing`
  (`frontend/src/lib/skyChart/geometry.ts`), and cite the line.
- **`edge_polyline(edge) -> list[tuple[float, float]]`:** the J2000 points
  for one edge, in its own direction.
  1. **Sample adaptively in t.** Split a piece at its midpoint while the
     precessed midpoint lies more than `SAMPLE_TOLERANCE_ARCSEC` from
     `linear_fill` of the piece's precessed ends. Stop at a minimum piece
     of 0.005° of the edge's length.
  2. **Simplify,** keeping both ends, so every sample lies within
     `SIMPLIFY_TOLERANCE_ARCSEC` (great-circle) of `linear_fill` between
     the kept points around it.
  3. **Round** to 3 decimals.

  Each edge is built once. A constellation that traverses it backwards
  uses the reversed list, so neighbours share exactly the same points.
- **`build_sky_data(package_dir, hyg_path, stellarium_index_path, star_names_path, *, allow_unpinned=False) -> dict`.**
  - The output keys are `source`, `stars`, `figures`, `boundaries`,
    `labels` and `names`, in that order.
  - `source` becomes `{"inputs": {<file name>: {"sha256": …, "origin": <url or package@version>}}}`.

  - `names` is `[[star_index, name], …]`, sorted by index, for each star
