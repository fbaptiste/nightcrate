# v0.52.0 — Sky chart on the project Info tab

Fred asked on 2026-11-13 for "a planisphere of the night sky with the
constellations, and an indicator of where in the night sky the project image
is located", and moved it to this version. He chose the design from a local
mockup, `.superpowers/mockups/v0.52.0/sky-chart.html` (ignored), which shows
three charts and four ways to draw constellations, with real sky data. Where
the mockup and this text differ, the text wins.

## Fred's decisions (2026-11-13)

- **Chart B, the atlas with pan and zoom** ("go with B"). It opens on the
  picture's constellation, like a star-atlas page. You can zoom out to the
  whole sky, or in until your own picture appears in place, sized and
  rotated as the plate solve measured it. It needs no location or date, so it
  works for every solved project.
  - Not chosen: A, a planisphere with a magnifier, and C, the sky above the
    project's location at a chosen time.
- **IAU stick figures, with lines that stop short of each star** ("IAU lines
  stopping short"), as printed atlases draw them.
  - Not chosen: H. A. Rey's figures, boundaries only, and lines running into
    the stars.
- **Pan and zoom** with a mouse, a trackpad, or fingers on an iPad. He had
  asked: "I think this will need pan/zoom capabilities as well, maybe?"
- **The sky data ships with the app.** There's no new download in Admin.
- **Tonight at a Glance:** the darkness card gets a title like the others
  ("give it a title so it looks consistent with the remaining ones").

## Why

The Info tab says which constellation a picture is in. It can't show where
that is: how the target sits among the constellation's stars, how big the
picture is against the sky, or what lies around it. A chart answers that at a
glance and helps when planning the next picture of the same region.

## Goals

- Show where a solved picture sits in the sky, with the stars and
  constellation figures an observer knows.
- Let people move from the constellation out to the whole sky, and in to
  their own picture drawn in place.
- Work with a mouse, a trackpad, a keyboard and touch, in both themes.
- Stay quick: pan and zoom keep up with the pointer on an iPad.
- Use only openly licensed data, credited.

## Non-goals

- The sky above a place at a time (chart C), horizons and rise or set times.
  The Planner covers those.
- Deep-sky objects, planets, the Moon or the Milky Way on the chart. The
  Milky Way outline in the mockup's data folder has no clear licence.
- Stars fainter than magnitude 6.
- Sky charts anywhere other than the project Info tab.
- Choosing figure styles in Settings.

## 1. Where it appears

The Info tab's right column, built in v0.51.0, becomes:

1. the constellation name and "The constellation at the centre of the
   picture" (unchanged);
2. **the compact chart** (new);
3. **"On the meridian at midnight around April 23"** (new, section 6);
4. the Centre, Field and Rotation list (unchanged);
5. **an "Open the sky chart" button** (new): outlined, primary colour, with
   an expand icon.

**The compact chart:**

- It fills the column's width (312 px on wide screens), at a 4:3 width to
  height ratio. When the column drops below the main content (under
  1,100 px), it is capped at 480 px wide.
- It shows the constellation view (section 3) without names, except the
  picture's constellation, and without buttons, legend or scale bar.
- Clicking or tapping it opens the chart. It is hidden from screen readers
  and isn't a tab stop; the button is the keyboard route. It shows a pointer
  cursor.

**The opened chart** is a full-screen dialog:

- **Header**, on the paper colour:
  - the project name (16 px, semibold);
  - "*targets* in *Constellation*" (13 px, secondary). For one or two
    targets, list them joined with "and"; for more, say "*n* targets". A
    project with no targets reads "Centred in *Constellation*";
  - a close button, labelled "Close the sky chart". Esc closes too.
- **The chart** fills the rest of the dialog.
- **Buttons** over its top-right corner, in a column: "Your picture",
  "Constellation", "Whole sky", then a row with + and −.
- **A scale bar** at the bottom left: a round number of degrees or
  arcminutes, at most 110 px long (60°, 30°, 20°, 10°, 5°, 2°, 1°, 30′, 10′,
  5′, 2′).
- **A legend strip** along the bottom, on the paper colour, with three
  entries:
  - the outline swatch: "Your picture's outline (its real size and
    rotation)";
  - "Constellation lines";
  - a dashed swatch: "*Constellation* boundary".

## 2. What the chart draws

Draw in this order, back to front:

1. **Sky background.**
2. **Grid:** lines of right ascension and declination, spaced for the zoom.
   When the chart's shorter side spans *S* degrees, use 15° × 10° for
   S > 80, 5° for S > 20, 1° for S > 6, and 15′ otherwise. The grid has no
   labels.
3. **The picture's constellation boundary:** the official IAU boundary,
   dashed (5 on, 3 off), 1.6 px. Other constellations' boundaries aren't
   drawn.
4. **Constellation figures:** straight lines between their stars, 1 px at
   60% opacity. The picture's constellation is 1.6 px at 95%, in the
   brighter figure colour.
   - **Stop short:** each end of a segment is trimmed by the drawn radius of
     its star plus 3 px. Each trim is at most 40% of the segment's length,
     so short segments keep their middle.
5. **Stars**, as filled dots:
   - radius = max(0.55, (6.4 − magnitude) × 0.62 × *k*) px, where
     *k* = clamp(84 / *S*, 0.5, 1.25);
   - the faintest magnitude shown is 4.5 when S ≥ 150, 5.2 when S > 80, and
     every star otherwise.
6. **The picture:**
   - **When its outline's longer edge is under 10 px:** a reticle, a circle
     (radius 12 px opened, 7 px compact) with four ticks outside it.
   - **From 10 px:** the outline, 1.6 px, in the target colour. Draw it
     through the outline's points (section 4), so a very wide field
     curves correctly.
   - **From 46 px:** the plate-solved picture itself, drawn opaque under
     the outline. Section 4 sets the conditions.
7. **Constellation names**, italic:
   - 11 px in the label colour;
   - the picture's constellation in 13 px medium, in the brighter label
     colour.
8. **The picture's label:** the project name, 12.5 px semibold, in the target
   colour with a 3 px halo in the sky colour. Place it to the right of the
   outline or reticle, and shorten names over 32 characters with "…".

**Which names show:**

- **Opened chart:** every constellation when S < 150. From 150 up, which
  includes the whole-sky view, only the rank-1 constellations from the data
  (about 22) and the picture's own.
- **Compact chart:** only the picture's constellation.
- No name may overlap the picture's marker or label. A name that would is
  hidden, except the picture's constellation, which moves aside instead.

**Projection:**

- Stereographic, centred on the view's centre, with north up there and east
  to the left, as the sky looks overhead:
  `d3.geoStereographic().rotate([-ra, -dec]).reflectX(true)`.
- Nothing is drawn more than 150° from the centre.
- For a span *S* across the shorter side *m* px, the scale is
  m / (2 tan(S/4)), because d3's stereographic radius is
  scale × tan(θ/2). The mockup's formula used tan(S/2) for a "field" that
  was really half the span, so its opening view was about twice as wide as
  intended: roughly 140° across for Ursa Major.

## 3. Views and controls (opened chart)

**Views:**

- **Constellation**, the opening view.
  - Fit the constellation's boundary and the picture's centre in the chart,
    centred, with an 8% margin on each side.
  - S stays between 20° and 120°.
  - Serpens has two parts (section 5); fit both.
- **Your picture:** centred on the picture's centre, with S = 3.5 × the
  picture's longer side, so its surroundings show.
- **Whole sky:** centred on the picture, S = 180°. On a wide screen the
  corners reach about 125° from the centre.
  - The mockup's whole sky spanned about 240°, which stretches the outer sky
    four times.
  - The thinning it showed (stars to magnitude 4.5, rank-1 names) starts
    at S = 150° here, so the whole-sky view keeps that look.
- **Zoom limits:** S from 0.1° (6′) to 180°.

**Moving around:**

- **Dragging moves the sky with the pointer.** The chart re-centres as it
  goes, so shapes don't stretch the way they would if the projection stayed
  centred on the opening view. The view's centre stops at the celestial
  poles.
- **Wheel, trackpad pinch and two-finger pinch** zoom about the pointer or
  the pinch midpoint; that point stays under the pointer or fingers.
- **Double-click or double-tap** zooms in by 2× at that point.
- **The view buttons** animate over 650 ms, interpolating the centre along
  the great circle and the scale logarithmically. With reduced motion
  preferred, they jump. + and − zoom by 2× about the centre over 250 ms.
- **Keyboard,** while the chart has focus:
  - the arrow keys pan by 10% of the shorter side;
  - + or = zooms in, − zooms out;
  - Home returns to the constellation view.
  - The chart takes focus when the dialog opens.
- **Touch** follows the tablet rules in `docs/development-decisions.md`
  ("Tablet and LAN behavior"):
  - `touch-action: none` on the chart, with touch listeners
    `{ passive: false }`;
  - Safari's `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` suppressed on
    the chart's container;
  - no callouts or text selection;
  - one finger pans, two fingers pinch.

## 4. The picture in place

The backend sends the plate solve's coordinate solution (the WCS). The
frontend works out where any pixel of the picture falls in the sky. It
draws the picture as a mesh of small triangles, and adds finer triangles
where you're zoomed in, so the picture stays aligned with the stars at
every zoom, wide field or narrow.

(Codex's plan review, 2026-11-13, in three rounds. A single transform
through three corners was 10 px off for a 15° picture far from the
centre. Rules for hiding the misaligned picture made it vanish as you
zoomed in. A fixed 8 × 8 mesh drifted 4 px at S = 2° and 80 px at
S = 0.1° inside a 40° field. Only an adaptive mesh fed by the WCS holds
everywhere.)

**Backend:**

- `SolveField` gains:
  - `solve_id: int`;
  - `wcs: SolveWcs`, holding `crval1`, `crval2`, `crpix1`, `crpix2`,
    `cd1_1`, `cd1_2`, `cd2_1`, `cd2_2`, `width` and `height`, exactly as
    stored in `project_solve`. All of them are `NOT NULL`;
  - `midnight_meridian_month: int` and `midnight_meridian_day: int`
    (section 6).
- **Convention:** the stored WCS is top-down rows with 1-based CRPIX, and
  the rendered solve images use the same rows (development decisions,
  "Plate solving"), so no flip is needed.

**Frontend:**

- **Pixel to sky:**
  - `tanPixelToSky(wcs, x, y)` mirrors the backend's
    `services/object_search.py:_tan_pixel_to_sky`. It takes zero-based
    pixel centres, so the picture's outer edges are at −0.5 and
    width − 0.5.
  - It is checked against values calculated independently with
    `astropy.wcs`.
  - The outline is the four edges, eight points each.
- **The image:**
  - Start with the solve image's `thumb_lg` variant (800 px), through the
    existing `projectSolveImageUrl` helper with the solve ID.
  - Switch to `full` (4,000 px) once the picture is drawn wider than
    800 device pixels.
  - Image pixel `u = (x + 0.5) × imageWidth / wcs.width`, and likewise for
    `v`.
  - A missing image (the route returns 204) or a failed load leaves the
    outline only, with no message.
- **Drawing:**
  - The picture is drawn once its outline's longer edge reaches 46 px.
  - **The adaptive mesh:**
    - Start from 8 × 8 cells.
    - A cell that is off screen, or that has a corner more than 120° from
      the view's centre, is skipped.
    - A visible cell is split into four when any of seven sample points
      lands more than 0.25 CSS px from where its two triangles would put
      it. The samples are both triangles' centres, the four edge midpoints
      and the diagonal's midpoint (Codex's plan review, round 4).
      Splitting goes at most 10 levels deep, with at most 4,096 triangles
      per frame.
  - **Each triangle:**
    - is drawn with its own transform;
    - is clipped to its projected triangle, with each edge pushed out by
      0.6 px (the miter capped at 3 px), so no seams show;
    - draws only the source rectangle that covers that grown clip, plus a
      2-pixel margin, keeping the image's own coordinates.

## 5. Sky data

> **Superseded in part (2026-11-14):** section 12 replaces the star and
> boundary sources, the Mira rule, the credits and the source terms below.

**Source:** the data files of d3-celestial 0.7.35 (npm). The package is
BSD-3-Clause, © 2015 Olaf Frohn. Its README names these sources:

| File | Content | Source named by d3-celestial |
|---|---|---|
| `stars.6.json` | 5,044 stars to magnitude 6, J2000 | XHIP: An Extended Hipparcos Compilation, Anderson & Francis (2012), VizieR V/137D |
| `constellations.lines.json` | IAU stick figures | The IAU constellation pages, with "some line modifications" by the author |
| `constellations.bounds.json` | IAU boundaries, J2000 | Catalogue of Constellation Boundary Data, Davenhall & Leggett (1989), VizieR VI/49 |
| `constellations.json` | Name positions and rank | The IAU constellation pages, with the author's positions |

**A generator** reads an unpacked copy of the package and writes the app's
data:

- **Location:** `backend/scripts/build_sky_data.py`, alongside the other
  scripts.
- **Input:** the package directory, passed as an argument. The script
  doesn't download anything.
- **Output:** one file, `frontend/src/data/sky/sky.json`, holding:
  - **stars:** `[ra, dec, mag]`, with RA from 0° to 360°, positions to four
    decimals (0.36″) and magnitudes to two, brightest first;
  - **figures:** for each constellation code, polylines given as indices
    into the stars;
  - **boundaries:** for each code, rings of `[ra, dec]` to three decimals.
    The source's vertices are sparse, and its edges follow lines of constant
    right ascension or declination. Drawn as straight great-circle arcs,
    some sag by up to 0.85°.
    - The file keeps the sparse vertices.
    - The chart fills in points, linearly in RA and Dec, no more than 0.25°
      apart, before drawing a boundary.
    - Filling in 1° steps in the generator made the file 352 KB (Codex's
      plan review), and left a visible sag at close zoom;
  - **labels:** for each code, one or more name points and the rank;
  - **source:** the package version, and the SHA-256 of each source file.

  It should stay under 250 KB before compression.
- **Joining lines to stars:** each line vertex snaps to a star within 0.01°,
  and the generator fails on any vertex it can't place. In 0.7.35, 892 of
  the 893 vertices match within 0.0085°.
  - The exception is in Cetus, at RA 34.8366°, Dec −2.9776°. That is Mira
    (ο Ceti, HIP 10826), a variable star missing from the magnitude-6 list.
  - The generator adds Mira from XHIP at its catalogue position and draws it
    as the faintest dot. That rule is written in the script with its reason.
- **Serpens** appears twice in each source file, both as `Ser`.
  - Keep both parts, labelled "Serpens Caput" and "Serpens Cauda". These
    names already exist in `lib/constellations.ts`.
  - Both parts count as the picture's constellation when the code is `Ser`.
- **Names on the chart** come from `CONSTELLATION_NAMES`, not the data. The
  backend's constellation (astropy) uses the same IAU boundaries.
- **The source files** aren't committed. The script's header says how to
  fetch the package (`npm pack d3-celestial@0.7.35`), and the output records
  the hashes.

**Loading:**

- The chart's code and data load lazily, in their own chunk, the first time
  an Info tab with a solved project renders.
- The main bundle grows by no more than the lazy-loading stub.
- While loading, the chart's box shows the sky colour.
- If loading fails, the box shows "The sky chart couldn't load. Reload the
  page to try again."; the rest of the tab is unaffected.
- If the opened chart fails to load, the dialog shows the same message and
  its close button.

  retry: React keeps a failed lazy import's error until the page reloads,
  and in a local app a failed chunk usually means the build changed under
  an open page.

## 6. "On the meridian at midnight around …"

This is the date on which the picture's centre crosses the meridian at local
solar midnight, opposite the Sun. Around then it's highest at the darkest
time of night. The date depends only on right ascension, so it's the same
for every location and hemisphere.

**Backend:**

- Add a pure `midnight_meridian_date(ra_deg: float, year: int) -> tuple[int,
  int]` to `services/astronomy.py`.
- It takes the Sun's geocentric right ascension (astropy `get_body("sun")`,
  GCRS) at 12:00 UTC on each day of `year`. It picks the day on which that
  value is closest, around the circle, to `ra_deg + 180°`.
- Cache the year's table. The endpoint passes the current UTC year.
- **Regression values for 2026**, checked against astropy and the usual
  almanac dates:

  | Picture centre | RA (°) | Expected |
  |---|---|---|
  | M 101 | 210.80 | 23 April |
  | M 42 | 83.82 | 16 December |
  | M 31 | 10.68 | 5 October |
  | Carina Nebula | 160.9 | 28 February |
  | RA 0° | 0.0 | 23 September |
  | RA 180° | 180.0 | 20 March |

**Frontend:** format the date as "April 23" in the app's usual date style.

## 8. Drawing performance

- **One canvas** draws everything except the buttons, scale bar and legend:
  - sized to its box times `devicePixelRatio`;
  - redrawn at most once per animation frame;
  - resized with a `ResizeObserver`.
- **Culling:** skip anything more than 150° from the centre or off the
  canvas.
- **Target:** in headless Chromium at 1,440 × 900 CSS px and a device pixel
  ratio of 2, the whole-sky view's median redraw takes under 16 ms. Measure
  it and report the result.

## 11. Star names (added 2026-11-14)

Fred asked for star names "when I view the large sky chart - not all the
stars, just the main bright ones", and approved this rule ("yes, add it to
v0.52.0 with that rule"):

- **The opened chart only.** The compact chart shows no star names.
- **Which stars:**
  - when S ≥ 150, which includes the whole-sky view: magnitude 1.5 or
    brighter (23 names: Sirius, Canopus, Arcturus, Vega…);
  - when S ≤ 90, which covers every constellation view (39–67° in a
    desktop window) and the picture view: magnitude 3.5 or brighter (186
    names in all). In Ursa Major that names all seven Big Dipper stars.
  - **Claude's call (rendered checks, 2026-11-14):** between 90 and 150 the
    limit falls linearly from 3.5 to 1.5. A step at 150 took the M 101
    view from 10 names to 98 in one zoom step. The ramp gives 10, 18, 28,
    38 and 53 names at 150, 135, 120, 105 and 90.
  - A name shows only when its star is drawn on the canvas.
- **Source: the IAU Catalog of Star Names** (IAU-CSN, from the IAU Working
  Group on Star Names), matched to stars by Hipparcos number. Use its
  `Name/Diacritics` column.
  - The input is `IAU-CSN.txt`, last updated 2022-04-04, from the WGSN
    secretary's page (`https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~emamajek/WGSN/IAU-CSN.txt`).
    Its SHA-256 is
    `84fac0c90f1b19abc491c2793469e0caa7b003ad1bc93a790ca41147010d0eb0`,
    recorded in `sky.json` like the other inputs.
  - The WGSN says the names belong to the public domain
    (`https://exopla.net/star-names/modern-iau-star-names/`). The file
    states that IAU products are released under Creative Commons
    Attribution.
- **Placement:** try to the right of the star, then left, above, and below,
  and take the first spot that overlaps nothing already placed.
  - The picture's marker and label, then the constellation names, are
    placed first. Star names follow, brightest first.
  - The marker's reserved area is the projected outline's bounding box
    (+3 px), or the whole reticle with its ticks. The picture's label sits
    7 px to the right of it, and the target constellation's fallback
    placement goes below it.
  - Names also avoid the opened chart's tool buttons and scale bar.
  - A name with no free spot is hidden.
- **Style:**
  - 10.5 px regular, upright, which tells them apart from the italic
    constellation names;
  - a warm grey (table below), with a 1.5 px halo in the sky colour (line
    width 3) so figure lines don't cut through the text.

**Stars:**
- Every HYG row with magnitude ≤ 6.0, after rounding to two decimals,
  except the Sun (`id` 0). RA is in hours in HYG (× 15).
- Rows at the same rounded position merge into the brightest. Alula
  Australis A and B are one example, and Graffias and its companion the
  other.
- A figure vertex snaps to the nearest kept star within 0.01°. Only if
  there's none is the nearest star of any magnitude added. This replaces
  section 5's Mira rule. Mira (HYG magnitude 6.47) is the only star added
  this way, for 5,069 stars in all.
- Rows without a Hipparcos number stay. There are 29 of them to
  magnitude 6, mostly components of double stars from the Gliese and Yale
  catalogues. One is Alula Australis (ξ UMa), which the Ursa Major figure
  needs.
- **Claude's check:** all 893 figure vertices then snap within 31″
  (0.0085°), as now. Positions agree with the stars shipped now to a median
  of 0.15″.

**Boundaries:**
- **Edge format:** each edge reads `a:b T ra1 dec1 ra2 dec2 CON1 CON2`.
  - `M+` runs along constant RA, and `P+` along constant Dec, east from
    ra1 to ra2, wrapping through 0h.
  - `SER1` and `SER2` are both `Ser`. The other codes map to d3-celestial's
    codes regardless of case.
- **Rings:** chain each constellation's edges through their shared vertex
  numbers. A ring that doesn't close, or a vertex that doesn't have exactly
  two of the constellation's edges, is an error.
- **Points:**
  - precess with astropy, from FK4 (equinox B1875) to FK5 (J2000);
  - sample each edge adaptively, so the chart's linear RA/Dec fill-in stays
    close to the true precessed line. Even 0.25° steps aren't enough near
    the pole: Codex measured 5″ on the Ursa Minor/Cepheus edge at 8h
    (plan review round 2);
  - build each shared edge once, and reverse it for its other
    constellation, so neighbours share exactly the same line;
  - round to three decimals.
- **Accuracy:** the line the chart draws stays within 6″ of the true
  precessed boundary everywhere. That covers sampling (1″), simplification
  (2″) and rounding (up to 2.6″).
- **Claude's check:** all 694 corners, precessed this way and before
  rounding, lie within 3.2″ of the J2000 boundaries shipped now (VizieR
  VI/49). The median is 1.3″. After rounding, one corner (Gem/Mon) is
  5.1″ away (Codex).

## Claude's calls, for Fred's review

- **A tighter opening view.** The mockup opened about twice as wide as
  intended (section 2). The chart opens fitting the constellation instead,
  like an atlas page.
- **Dragging re-centres the chart,** so another part of the sky never looks
  stretched.
- **The whole sky is a hemisphere across** (180°), not the mockup's 240°.
  Its thinning (fewer stars and names) still applies.
- **The picture's constellation is drawn brighter, with its IAU boundary
  dashed,** so you can see where it ends. No other boundaries are drawn.
- **Night style in the dark theme and Paper in the light theme,** with no
  separate setting.
- **The compact chart is a picture;** pan and zoom live in the opened chart,
  where there's room.
- **The picture is drawn as an adaptive mesh from the WCS,** so it lines
  up with the stars at any size, position and zoom, as Codex recommended.
  Only parts very far from the view's centre are skipped.
- **Every star a figure needs is kept** (section 12), so Cetus's figure
  stays whole with Mira. This replaces the earlier rule that added Mira
  by hand.
- **The midnight date comes from astropy,** so M 101 reads 23 April, not the
  mockup's 20 April.
- **The darkness card's title is "Darkness",** and its labels lose their
  capitals to match the other cards.
- **Star names thin out gradually** between a 90° and a 150° view, instead
  of jumping from 10 to about 100 at 150° (section 11).
- **Names keep clear of the chart's buttons and scale bar,** and the
  picture's label sits beside a rotated outline instead of across its
  corner.
- **Star names come from the IAU's own list,** not d3-celestial's
  `starnames.json`. Compared on 2026-11-14 down to magnitude 3.5, that file
  has 30 names the IAU hasn't adopted (Regor, Navi, Mula and others). It
  also gives ζ Centauri the name of α Gruis (Alnair), and keeps two older
  spellings: Shedar for Schedar, and Unurgunite for Nganurganity.
