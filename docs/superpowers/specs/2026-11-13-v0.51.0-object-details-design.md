## Why

The Gallery shows pictures and how they were made, but not what they show.
Astrophotographers want to know which constellation an object is in, its
catalog numbers, how far away it is and where to read about it. The distance
gives a sense of scale that a picture alone cannot: whether the light left a
nebula in our own galaxy or a galaxy hundreds of millions of years ago.

## Goals

- The same facts, drawn the same way, on Gallery cards, the list view, the
  full-size viewer (Gallery and Messier pages) and the project Info tab.
- One distance ruler that reads at a glance, handles one target or dozens,
  marks estimates, and places the other catalogued objects in the picture
  behind the targets.
- Every distance added or changed in this version is either a cited
  measurement or a labelled estimate; distances that mislead are removed.
  A wrong distance is worse than none.
- No new database tables or migrations.

## Non-goals

- The sky chart (v0.52.0). The Info tab's right column leaves room for it.
- Editing distances or Wikipedia links in the app.
- Wikipedia in languages other than English (the catalog stores English
  links only).
- Distances for objects outside Messier and Caldwell that have none.
- Reworking the imaging facts on the project Overview or Sessions tabs.

## 1. Facts for each object

These rules apply everywhere the facts appear.

- **Name**: the common name when the object has one, otherwise its primary
  designation (`Pinwheel Galaxy`; `M 65`).
- **Catalog numbers**: its Messier and NGC designations, Messier first. When
  it has neither, its primary designation (`IC 1805`, `Sh2-129`). Omit any
  number already used as the name. Examples: Pinwheel Galaxy → `M 101,
  NGC 5457`; M 65 → `NGC 3623`; Heart Nebula → `IC 1805`.
- **Type**: the app's existing type names (`displayDsoType`), with HII regions
  shown as `Emission nebula` (the existing `filterTypeCode` merge).
- **Constellation**: the full name (`displayConstellation`; Serpens Caput and
  Cauda as today).
- **Type line**: one target → `Galaxy in Ursa Major`. Several targets in one
  constellation → `All in Leo`. Several constellations → `In Andromeda and
  Triangulum` (`In A, B and C` for three or more).
- **Also known as** (Info tab only): the object's other designations and names
  (the Gallery's existing `also_known_as`), minus the name and catalog numbers.
- **Wikipedia**: the object's English Wikipedia link from the catalog's
  external references. Opens in a new tab (`target="_blank"`,
  `rel="noopener noreferrer"`). Clicking it never turns a card or the viewer.
  No link when the catalog has none.
- **Distance**:
  - Source: the catalog's `distance_pc`. A distance whose method is
    `redshift` is an **estimate**; curated and 50 MGC distances are not.
  - Light-years = parsecs × 3.26156, to three significant figures, using the
    existing `distanceFormat.ts` rounding.
  - Wording: under a million, the number with thousands separators
    (`444`, `2,610`, `87,300`); then `22.7 million`, `36.0 million`,
    `1.25 billion`. Keep the trailing zero (`36.0`).
  - Long form `22.7 million light-years`; short form `22.7 million ly` where
    space is tight (card and list rulers).
  - Estimates read `about 245 million light-years (estimate)` where there is
    room; `about 245 million ly` in tight spots.
  - No distance: the distance is simply absent; see section 2 for the ruler.

## 2. The distance ruler

One shared component, drawn at five sizes: small card, medium/large card,
list row, full-size viewer, Info tab. The mockups show each.

- **Scale**: logarithmic, from 100 to 10 billion light-years, the same on
  every ruler so rulers compare at a glance. Each decade has a tick.
  Distances outside the scale sit at its nearest end.
- **Band**: the part from 100 to 100,000 light-years is shaded in the
  secondary slate blue (`#5b7a9e` light / `#7d9ab8` dark, low alpha as in the
  mockups). It is a size reference: the Milky Way is about 100,000 light-years
  across.
  - Info tab: a thin bracket under the tick labels spans the band, with the
    caption `Milky Way: about 100,000 light-years across` beneath it.
  - Cards, list and viewer: the caption sits in the label row under the band
    (`… ly across` on cards and list; `… light-years across` in the viewer).
  - Small cards: no caption and no tick labels.
- **Tick labels**:
  - Info tab: every decade, `100 light-years`, `1,000`, … `10 billion`.
  - Viewer: `1 million` and `10 billion light-years`; the middle label is
    hidden at phone width.
  - List: `1 million` and `10 billion ly`.
  - Medium/large cards: `10 billion` at the right end.
- **Your targets** sit on the line as amber dots (theme primary).
- **Other objects found in the plate-solved picture** sit on a lane just
  above the line, as smaller grey dots (`text.secondary`, reduced opacity).
  "Found" means the catalog objects the project's plate solve stored for its
  picture (`project_dso` for the project's `project_solve`), minus the
  targets. Only those with a distance are drawn.
- **Markers that would overlap merge** into one larger dot with the count
  inside, separately for targets and for other objects (the Info mockup's
  `groups()` rule: a dot joins a group when it is within about one marker
  width of the group's first dot). Targets draw above other objects.
- **Estimates**: a single target whose distance is an estimate is a hollow
  amber ring; a single other object with an estimated distance is a hollow
  grey ring. A merged marker is always filled; its count says how many.
- **Value label**, above the markers: the targets' distance, or their range
  when they differ: `36.0–38.9 million ly` when both ends share a unit,
  `7.54 million – 1.25 billion ly` otherwise. A single estimate adds `about`
  (and `(estimate)` where there is room). The label stays inside the ruler:
  left-aligned near the left end, right-aligned near the right end, centred
  otherwise.
- **Legend** (list, viewer, Info): `Your targets` (or `Your target`); the
  hollow ring as `Estimate worked out from redshift` when any hollow ring
  (amber or grey) is drawn; the
  grey dot as `Other objects found in the plate-solved picture` when some are
  drawn; the count marker as `Several at about the same distance` when a
  merge happened. Cards show only the grey-dot line, as
  `16 other objects found in the plate-solved picture` (counting drawn dots).
- **No distances**:
  - If no target has a distance but other objects do, draw the ruler with
    the grey markers and, in place of the value label,
    `No distance on record for your target` (or `targets`).
  - If nothing has a distance, show `No distance on record.` instead of the
    ruler.
- **Hover**: on the viewer and the Info tab, hovering a target row or an
  objects-table row raises its marker (or the merged marker that contains
  it), as in the mockups. Not needed on cards.
- **Accessibility**: the ruler is a figure with a text alternative listing
  each target's distance; the visible value label and the lists carry the
  same facts in text. Shape and position, not colour alone, separate targets
  (on the line) from other objects (on the lane), and estimates (hollow) from
  measurements.

## 3. Gallery card backs

Per the `card-details.html` and `deep-field.html` mockups.

- **Up to four targets**: one row per target with the name in bold, the
  catalog numbers after it in secondary text, and a **W** badge on the
  right. The W is a serif letter in a small rounded square, linking to
  Wikipedia, with the aria-label `<name> on Wikipedia (opens in a new tab)`.
  A single target's name is larger (17 px on medium cards). Then the type
  line, then the ruler.
- **More than four targets**: a count line (`21 galaxies` when every target
  is a galaxy, otherwise `21 targets`), then the first three names
  `and 18 more`, with the type line folded in (`…, all in Virgo`), then the
  ruler.
- **Your imaging**, below a hairline, in two lines.
  - First: `Imaged ` plus the latest run as today's formatter writes it
    (`formatImagingRuns`: months such as `Jan–Feb 2026`, or exact dates
    for a single one-month run), with the existing `+1 earlier` pill. The
    mockups' day ranges are illustrative only: runs are stored by month.
  - Second: `5 nights, 11 h 20 m, Refractor`. Rigs: one or two
  names joined by "and"; three or more as the first name and `and 2 more`.
  Missing values use today's wording (`No imaging dates`, and so on).
- **Status pills and the Open project button** as today.
- **Small cards**: the designations on one line (`M 65, M 66, NGC 3628`),
  the ruler without labels, and the latest imaging run. No W badges: there
  is no room for something easy to tap.
- **Overflow**: a long back scrolls, with the bottom fade cards already have.

## 4. Full-size viewer: the details side

Per `card-details.html` (and `deep-field.html` for many targets). Applies on
the Gallery and Messier pages.

- **Turning over**: while the slideshow is paused, a click or tap on the
  picture turns it over to the details side; a click or tap on the details
  (outside a link or button) turns it back. The 400 ms turn matches the
  cards; with reduced motion it is instant.
- **While playing**, a tap shows or hides the controls as today and never
  turns the picture.
- **Keys**:
  - `Enter`, with focus on the viewer rather than a button, turns the
    picture over or back.
  - `Esc` turns the details side back first, then closes the viewer.
  - `←`, `→`, the side arrows and swipes go to the next picture, picture
    side up.
  - `Space` or the play button turns the picture back and starts playing.
- **The details side**: a dark panel (the dark palette, whatever the app
  theme) centred in the picture area, up to 600 px wide. It contains:
  - the target rows, type line and ruler, with the viewer's labels and
    legend;
  - with more than four targets, the count, the ruler and a scrolling list
    of all targets, nearest first, each with its distance and W badge, as in
    `deep-field.html`;
  - your imaging, with every run listed rather than just the latest;
  - the Open project button.
- **Scrolling**: the panel scrolls vertically when it is taller than the
  picture area, which happens on phones and short screens. Touch scrolling
  inside it must work (the picture area today blocks all touch gestures), and
  a sideways swipe still changes pictures.
- **Accessibility**: the hidden side is `aria-hidden` and out of the tab
  order. The picture area is a button named `Show details for <title>` or
  `Show the picture`.
- **The Messier page**: its viewer entries know only the project, so the
  details side loads the project's details when it turns over, with a small
  progress indicator, and an error line if loading fails.

## 5. Gallery list view

Per `gallery-list.html`. Each row keeps the thumbnail, the project name and

- **Left, the objects**: the target rows (name, catalog numbers, W badge),
  the type line, the ruler with grey dots, and the grey-dot line. More than
  four targets: the count summary as on cards.
- **Right, your imaging**: today's Imaging dates, Integration and Rigs rows,

## 6. The project Info tab

Per `project-info.html`.

- **Position**: Overview, Sessions, **Info**, Files, Plate Solve, Notes. The
  page's tab numbering changes. `?tab=plate-solve` keeps working, and
  `?tab=info` opens the new tab. Any code that switches to a tab by number,
  such as Notes opening a night on Sessions, must still land on the right tab.
- **Not plate solved**: the heading
  `Info appears once this project is plate solved`, the sentence
  `Plate solving one of this project's pictures tells NightCrate exactly
  what's in it. This tab then shows your targets with their catalog
  numbers, constellation, distance and Wikipedia links, plus the other
  catalogued objects in the picture.`, and a **Plate solve it** button that opens the
  Plate Solve tab.
- **Plate solved**: two columns at up to 1280 px (`minmax(0, 1fr)` and
  340 px), one column below 1100 px.
- **Left column**:
  - **Target / Targets**: one ruled row per target, with:
    - the name (20 px) and catalog numbers;
    - the type line;
    - `Also known as …`;
    - on the right, the distance (long form) and a `Wikipedia ↗` text link.

    No distance on record reads `No distance on record`.
  - **How far away**: the full-size ruler with every tick labelled, the
    bracket and caption, and the legend.
  - **Also in this picture**: `N catalogued objects, nearest first`, then a
    table of the other objects in the plate solve, with columns Object, Type,
    Distance and W. Objects without a distance come last and read
    `no distance`. The table shows ten rows, then `Show all N` and
    `Show fewer`. The section is absent when there are none.
- **Right column, "In the sky"**:
  - the constellation at the centre of the picture, from the solve's centre
    (`constellation_for_coords`), in large type, captioned
    `The constellation at the centre of the picture`;
  - the solve's centre in RA and Dec, the field in degrees (`2.09° × 1.05°`)
    and the rotation.

  The v0.52.0 sky chart goes in this column. Nothing in this version
  mentions it on screen.
- **Data**: the tab loads one project-details response. The Messier viewer
  (section 4) uses the same one. A single backend function builds a
  project's target facts and found objects for both this endpoint and the
  Gallery response, so the two cannot drift apart.

## 7. Distances for Messier and Caldwell objects

113 objects (56 Messier, 57 Caldwell) have no distance, mostly star clusters.
About 30 Messier and Caldwell objects already have curated distances with
mostly uncited notes, and some differ from the modern catalogs (M 13:
7.2 kpc curated, 7.42 kpc in Baumgardt & Vasiliev 2021). So the existing
curated rows for Messier and Caldwell **clusters and planetary nebulae** are
re-sourced from the same catalogs too, and one class of object never mixes
sources. Existing nebula rows keep their values, with one exception: NGC 2237, a
part of the Rosette Nebula, moves from 1550 to 1420 pc so that it matches
C 49 at the NGC 2244 cluster distance. Citing the other nebula rows is a
follow-up. Claude compiles the distances from published catalogs, through the Harvard VizieR
mirror while CDS Strasbourg is down, and checks them before they are added:

- Globular clusters: Baumgardt & Vasiliev 2021 (MNRAS 505, 5957; VizieR
  J/MNRAS/505/5957), Gaia EDR3.
- Open clusters: Hunt & Reffert 2023 (A&A 673, A114; VizieR J/A+A/673/A114),
  Gaia DR3; Cantat-Gaudin et al. 2020 for any it lacks.
- Planetary nebulae: a Gaia-parallax compilation (González-Santamaría et al.
  2021, or as cited).
- The rest: one published value each, cited.
- Chance groupings with no single distance, such as M 40 (an optical double)
  and M 73 (an asterism), get none, with the reason recorded.

The rows are appended to `dso_augment.csv` as curated distances, each with
its citation in `notes`. The loader reapplies the file when its hash changes,
so existing users get the distances on their next start. Bump `version.json`.
README acknowledgments credit the catalogs (facts from published catalogs,
cited; CDS/VizieR terms ask for acknowledgment). The raw tables and the
compilation report stay in `.superpowers/tmp/v051/distances/` and are not
committed.

## 8. Redshift estimates that mislead

Two distances in a real Virgo field turned out wrong:

- **NGC 4452**, a Virgo cluster member at about 55 million ly, shows
  7.54 million ly. Its redshift is real (162 km/s), but its own motion inside
  the cluster swamps its expansion velocity, so the Hubble-law estimate
  means nothing.
- **IC 3418** (VCC 1217), a Virgo dwarf, shows 1.25 billion ly. OpenNGC and
  SIMBAD carry z = 0.0896, while NED gives z = 0.000567 (Jáchym et al. 2013).
  The large value evidently belongs to another source.

Changes:

- **Threshold.** The redshift backfill skips galaxies with z < 0.01
  (cz < about 3,000 km/s). At that range, galaxies' own motions are a large
  fraction of their recession velocity. 49 galaxies lose a distance; none is
  better than a misleading one.
- **IC 3418.** A curated row gives it the Virgo cluster distance (16.5 Mpc,
  Mei et al. 2007, as 50 MGC uses for its Virgo members), citing NED's
  redshift in the note.
- **Records.** The development decisions note the threshold and why. The
  OpenNGC redshift for IC 3418 goes in Fred's notes as a possible upstream
  report.

## 9. Forecast: shorter wait for a hung server

On 2026-11-13, Open-Meteo's US West server (`51.81.167.44`) hung on new
connections (open-meteo/open-meteo#2329). NightCrate waited 30 s, retried,
and waited another 30 s before falling back to the saved forecast, about a
minute in all.

- Every Open-Meteo request in `services/weather.py` passes
  `connect_timeout=10.0` to `http_client.get`: forecast, archive, cloud
  models, model metadata, ECMWF water vapour, and air quality.
- A hung server then costs about 21 s. Slow downloads keep their 30 s
  budget, and the retry and the fallback are unchanged.
- A test asserts each call passes the connect limit.

## Claude's calls, for Fred's review

These were made while Fred was away (2026-11-13), as best guesses.

- **No imaging details on the Info tab.** It covers what's in the picture;
  dates, integration and rigs stay on Overview and Sessions.
- **Which plate solve.** The Info tab and grey dots use the project's one
  plate solve (`project_solve` allows one per project). It can come from any
  of the project's pictures, not necessarily the Gallery's main one.
- **Existing cluster and PN distances re-sourced** from the same modern
  catalogs, which changes some values users already see (M 13 from about
  23,500 to 24,200 light-years). Other nebula rows are unchanged, except
  NGC 2237, which takes C 49's cluster distance.
- **Grey estimates are hollow** like amber ones, so a redshift estimate is
  never drawn like a measurement.
- **Many targets.** The count summary starts above four targets.
- **Redshift threshold** at z < 0.01, removing 49 misleading estimates
  (section 8).
- **IC 3418** gets the Virgo cluster distance from a curated row.
- **Forecast connection limit** of 10 s.
- **Rigs on cards**: two names, then `and N more`.
- **The W badge** stands for Wikipedia on cards, list and viewer; the Info tab
  spells out `Wikipedia`.
- **Cards count only drawn dots** in the grey-dot line. Objects without a
  distance appear only in the Info tab's table.
- **The Info tab's right column** shows the constellation at the picture's
  centre and the solve's centre, field and rotation now; the sky chart joins
  it in v0.52.0.
- **Calls made during implementation and review:**
  - **Small cards show no grey dots.** There isn't room for the lane above
    the line and the imaging date.
  - **The Info ruler's first tick reads `100 ly`.** The long form ran into
    `1,000`.
  - **Four objects get no distance:** M 24, M 40, M 73 and C 37. Each is a
    chance grouping or has no single published distance.
  - **C 50's distance is keyed by NGC 2244.** OpenNGC folds NGC 2239 into
    it, though some papers treat NGC 2239 as a separate background cluster.
  - **NGC 2237 now shares C 49's cluster distance** (1420 pc instead of
    1550), so the Rosette reads as one object.
  - **Four planetary nebulae move a lot** with their modern Gaia
    distances: NGC 3242 from 550 to 1280 pc, NGC 2392 from 900 to 1730,
    NGC 6543 from 1000 to 1320 and NGC 2438 from 1400 to 849.
