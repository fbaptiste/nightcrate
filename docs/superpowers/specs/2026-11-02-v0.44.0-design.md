# v0.44.0 design: more deep-sky catalogs and search links

**Status:** written on 2026-11-02 from Fred's decisions in the brainstorm,
checked by a separate builder review, and approved by Fred on 2026-11-02.
Fred removed the supernova remnant catalog the same day, when approving the
plan (§5.12).
Decisions marked **Fred** are his. The rest are marked **Claude's call**:
§9 lists them, Fred read them before approving, and Codex reviews them in
the plan discussion.

## 1. Why

NightCrate loads only OpenNGC, Sharpless and Barnard in full. Other catalogs
appear only as alternate names on those objects, so popular targets with no
NGC, IC or Sharpless number cannot be found:
- LBN 437 (the Gecko Nebula) and LBN 777 (the Vulture Head);
- the Alpha Persei cluster (Melotte 20). "Melotte 25" didn't find the Hyades
  either, although NightCrate has it: only "Mel 25" worked;
- WR 134 and its bubble;
- the Cygnus Loop as a whole, rather than the Veil's separate NGC pieces.

Melotte 15, which is IC 1805, returns nothing either: NightCrate doesn't know
that name. All of these were checked on 2026-11-01.

**Fred (2026-11-02):** NightCrate serves astrophotographers in general, not
just him. All ten catalogs are in scope, whether or not he uses them
himself.

**Success:** a user can:
- download any of the catalogs;
- find each of its objects by its catalog name;
- plan it, and link it to a project or the wishlist;
- remove the catalog later without losing those links.

## 2. Goals

1. **Load ten catalogs in full** (Fred, 2026-11-01): LBN, Melotte, LDN,
   Abell planetary nebulae, ACO (Abell galaxy clusters), Arp, Herbig-Haro,
   PN G, MCG and Wolf-Rayet stars. The facts are in §6. Supernova remnants
   were removed (Fred, 2026-11-02; §5.12).
2. **Each catalog is optional** (Fred, 2026-11-01).
   - The user downloads only the catalogs they want, each on its own from
     the Catalogs page, and can remove one later.
   - **Sharpless and Barnard become optional and removable** in the same way
     (Fred, 2026-11-02).
   - **OpenNGC stays required** (Fred, 2026-11-02). Messier, NGC, IC and
     Caldwell come from it, and every other catalog is matched against it.
3. **Search links** (Fred, 2026-11-01). Google and Google Images links join
   SIMBAD and Wikipedia in the catalog and planner object panels, as
   Telescopius offers.

## 3. Non-goals and anti-goals

**Non-goals:**
- Shipping VizieR rows inside NightCrate. Each user downloads at runtime, as
  Sharpless and Barnard work today (§7.7). Melotte is the exception (§5.7).
- The 2026 Herbig-Haro catalogue (1,193 objects): its CC BY-NC-ND licence
  makes it unusable.
- Crowther's current Wolf-Rayet catalogue (718 stars): it has no licence and
  no download.
- New planner scoring for faint objects. The visible limits in §5.8 are the
  only planner change.

**Anti-goals.** Each of these is a failure, even if the feature otherwise
works:
- An object wrongly folded into another and hidden.
- The same object listed twice when a catalog or a reviewed list names the
  match.
- A project target or wishlist entry lost when a catalog is removed.
- A duplicate created when a removed catalog is downloaded again, beside the
  linked objects that were kept.
- A failed or missing download stopping the other catalogs from loading.
- The catalog page or the planner becoming noticeably slower. All ten
  catalogs add up to about 36,000 new objects at most (§6) to the roughly 14,000 in a
  typical database today.

## 4. Stages (Claude's call)

One version, built in stages, so the shared parts are proven before any
catalog depends on them:
1. **Shared parts:**
   - optional and removable catalogs, with Sharpless and Barnard converted;
   - the "part of" relation (§5.1);
   - the new names and object types (one migration);
   - the visible planner limits (§5.8);
   - the search links.
2. **Catalogs, simplest first.**
   - Recommended order: Wolf-Rayet, Herbig-Haro, PN G with Abell
     planetary nebulae, LBN, LDN, Melotte, Arp, ACO, then MCG.
   - The large and grouped catalogs come last. The plan may reorder them,
     with reasons.

## 5. Decisions

### 5.1 One entry covering several objects (Fred, 2026-11-02)

A search for the entry's name brings up every object it covers. Each of those
objects shows that it is part of the entry, both in its details and among its
other names.
- **Arp 244** lists NGC 4038 and NGC 4039, each marked as part of Arp 244.
- **The Cygnus Loop** lists the Veil's pieces, each marked as part of the
  Cygnus Loop.

This needs a membership relation of its own. Today a designation belongs to
exactly one object: `dso_designation` is `UNIQUE (catalog, identifier)`.

### 5.2 The whole as well as its pieces (Fred, 2026-11-02)

When the catalog gives the whole entry its own position and size, the whole
also appears as an object beside its pieces. LBN 191 gives the Cygnus Loop
210′ × 160′, so the whole loop can be framed in the planner.

A small group such as the Antennae shows only its members (Claude's
playback, confirmed by Fred).

### 5.3 An entry that is one existing object (Fred, 2026-11-02; as today)

The entry becomes another name on that object, not a second copy. For
example, LBN 667 becomes a pill on IC 1848, and Melotte 15 a pill on IC 1805.

### 5.4 An entry with no match (as today)

It becomes a new object, such as the Gecko (LBN 437).

### 5.5 How a match is decided (Fred, 2026-11-02)

Entries merge only when one of these says they are the same object:
- the catalog itself (233 LDN rows name a Barnard object);
- OpenNGC's alternate names;
- one of NightCrate's reviewed lists, like today's
  `sharpless_crossref.csv`.

Position alone never merges.
- For catalogs that name no matches, such as most of LBN, a position search
  finds candidates. They are reviewed before any goes into a list.
- Dark nebulae stay separate from emission nebulae on the same line of
  sight. Today the Horsehead (B 33) stays apart from IC 434 behind it.

The cost is an occasional near-duplicate until the match is added to a list.

**A reviewed list can overrule a stated match (Fred, 2026-11-02, "let's try
that for now").** The builder check found a stated match that is wrong.
- LBN 191 covers the whole Cygnus Loop, 210′ × 160′.
- The LBN catalogue's Name column says NGC 6960, and OpenNGC lists LBN 191
  among NGC 6960's names. NGC 6960 is only the Western Veil.

The rules for such cases:
- NightCrate's reviewed lists take precedence over a match a catalog or
  OpenNGC states, so LBN 191 becomes the whole Cygnus Loop, with the Veil's
  pieces as its parts.
- Before stated matches are trusted, pairs whose sizes differ a lot are
  flagged and reviewed. This covers the 93 LBN, 7,553 MCG, 129 PN G and 233
  LDN-to-Barnard pairs.

### 5.6 Which catalogs are removable (Fred, 2026-11-02)

- OpenNGC: required.
- Sharpless, Barnard and the ten new catalogs: optional and removable.

### 5.7 Melotte ships as NightCrate's own reviewed list (Fred, 2026-11-02)

No downloadable Melotte table exists. The only full list is Wikipedia's, under
CC BY-SA, so NightCrate does not copy that table.
- NightCrate ships its own list instead, in `data/catalogs/nightcrate/`
  (MIT, like the other files there).
- Each Melotte number's identity is checked.
  - **Found when building the list (2026-11-02):** SIMBAD knows only 75
    numbers, so the list comes from Melotte's 1915 catalogue itself, with
    every NGC and IC row checked against OpenNGC's position.
  - 240 of the 245 name objects NightCrate already has. That includes the
    Pleiades, the Hyades and the Coma Star Cluster, which OpenNGC's
    addendum already provides.
  - Five are new clusters, such as the Alpha Persei cluster (Mel 20). Their
    positions come from Hunt & Reffert 2023 or SIMBAD, as recorded in the
    list.
- It stays optional, like the other catalogs.

### 5.8 Planner: the limits show what is applied (Fred, 2026-11-02)

**Today the planner misleads.**
- Tonight mode's filter row shows "Brighter than mag 12.0" and
  "Min size: 5′" greyed out, with their boxes unticked (`PlannerPage.tsx`).
- With the boxes unticked, the server still applies both limits. The values
  come from Settings: `planner_max_magnitude` 12 and
  `planner_min_size_arcmin` 5 (`api/planner.py`).
- Anytime mode applies no limits.

The row therefore says the limits are off while they're on. That also hides
Wolf-Rayet stars, ACO clusters and Herbig-Haro objects, which have no size,
with no visible reason.

**The fix (Fred):**
- Tonight mode opens with both boxes ticked, at the Settings values. What
  the row shows is what's applied.
- Unticking a box really turns that limit off. The server no longer applies
  a limit the page doesn't show.
- **Picking or clearing a catalog never changes the limits (Fred).** They
  stay as they were, and the user changes them if they want to.

**Unknown values (Claude's call).** A ticked limit treats an unknown value
the way the hidden default does today:
- an unknown brightness passes;
- an unknown size fails.

So tonight's default list stays as it is now:
- Sharpless regions and other nebulae without a magnitude still appear;
- Wolf-Rayet stars and other objects without a size appear once Min size is
  unticked.

Today a magnitude limit the user ticks drops unknown brightnesses. Under
this rule they pass instead, so a ticked box behaves the same whether it was
ticked by default or by the user.

The faint MCG galaxies stay out of tonight's default list because of the
magnitude and size limits.

### 5.9 Removing a catalog (Fred, 2026-11-01)

- The catalog's names are removed, along with the objects only it provides.
- Objects the user has linked stay, inactive, with their names. Linked means
  a project target (`project_target`) or the wishlist (`favorite_target`).
- **A solve's in-frame list is not a link (Claude's call).** `project_dso`
  is computed when the solve is made, and its main-target flag comes from
  `project_target` (`api/project_solve.py`). Its rows for a removed object
  go with that object. `user_links` keeps moving these rows during merges.
  - **Why:** with MCG loaded, every galaxy-field solve lists many MCG
    galaxies. Counting them as links would keep thousands of inactive
    objects after MCG is removed.
- An object that another loaded catalog also provides stays, with that
  catalog's entry. For example, LBN 667 is IC 1848.
- Downloading the catalog again brings the kept objects back to life, with
  the same identity and links, rather than creating copies.

### 5.10 The choice of catalogs applies to every database (Claude's call)

Today, downloaded catalog files live in one app-wide folder
(`registry.user_catalogs_root()`). Each workspace database loads every file
it finds when it is created or opened (`_initialize_database` in
`api/admin.py`).
- The user's catalog choice is stored app-wide, beside the files. This
  covers what is installed, what was removed, and whether Melotte is on.
- A database opened later applies the same choice. A removal there follows
  §5.9, without asking again, since links are kept anyway.
- **Why:** the files are already shared, and per-database choices would
  need every database to track a catalog it never loaded.

### 5.11 Solves made before a catalog was added (Claude's call)

Existing solves are not recomputed when a catalog is added. Someone who
downloads LBN won't see the Gecko in the in-frame list of a solve made
earlier. The roadmap's object search computes in-frame membership when it
is asked for, which covers this later.

### 5.12 Downloaded catalogs in tonight's list (Claude's call)

Objects from a downloaded catalog appear in tonight's list under the same
limits as everything else, as Sharpless regions already do. Many LBN entries
have a size and no magnitude, so they pass. A size estimated from an LDN
area is an estimate and doesn't count toward the size limit.

**Supernova remnants removed (Fred, 2026-11-02).** Green's VizieR table has
no column or flag for optical detection; only the web version has a
free-text "Optical:" note. Remnants seen only in radio would have joined
tonight's list. Told this, Fred removed the catalog when approving the plan.
The Cygnus Loop's whole comes from LBN 191 instead (§5.2).

**Names (Claude's call):**
- **Display forms:**
  - ACO 2151, which can't clash with Abell 21, a planetary nebula;
  - WR 134;
  - HH 46;
  - PN G000.0-01.3.
- **Search keys must handle structured names**
  (`_common.normalize_designation` strips spaces, `-` and `_`):
  - Galactic names differ only by the sign of the latitude
    (PN G000.0+02.0 and PN G000.0-02.0);
  - catalogs pad numbers differently: Arp's index writes MCG-02-05-50 for
    MCG-02-05-050.

  (The earlier WR 2-1 example was wrong: III/215 has no such names.)
- **Vocabulary:** the closed CHECK vocabularies need a new forward migration
  (the next is 0066):
  - catalog codes for Herbig-Haro, ACO, PN G and Wolf-Rayet;
  - object types for Herbig-Haro object, galaxy cluster and Wolf-Rayet star.

  The loader and model changes come with it. `abell`, `arp`, `lbn`, `ldn`, `mcg` and `mel` as catalog codes.

## 7. Constraints from the current code

1. **Object identity** (`docs/dso-catalog-architecture.md`).
   - Rows are matched by `(source_catalog_id, source_key)` and updated in
     place.
   - A source that creates objects needs a pure collector in
     `reconcile.build_object_set`'s ownership order. Follow "When to add a
     new catalog source" in that document.
   - Ownership is own-name-first, then first-owner-wins.
2. **Removed is not missing.** Today, a previously loaded object input whose
   file is missing defers the whole object step. A catalog the user removed
   must be a persisted state of its own:
   - it doesn't defer anything;
   - a missing file still defers, as today.
3. **Unclaimed rows today become inactive and are never deleted.** Removal
   (§5.9) deletes the unlinked objects only the removed catalog provided.
   Linked ones stay inactive.
4. **New tables.** Any new table that references `dso`, such as the
   membership relation in §5.1, must be classified in
   `catalog_loader.user_links`. The fresh-database guard test enforces this.
5. **Fetching.** VizieR catalogs use the existing mirror fetcher
   (`catalog_loader/vizier.py`): one pass over Strasbourg, Harvard and IDIA,
   with `version.json` as the commit marker. MCG is two tables.
6. **Catalogs page.**
   - It is `CatalogsAdminSection.tsx`, with per-source fetch endpoints in
     `api/admin.py`.
   - It already shows each fetch's step, seconds and bytes, and runs one
     catalog action at a time (v0.43.1).
   - Removal follows the same rules.
7. **Terms and credits.**
   - VizieR data is "free of usage in a scientific context" with the authors
     cited. None of the ReadMes states a copyright.
   - Each source is credited in README's acknowledgments and the
     attribution panel.
8. **Search links.** They belong in `DsoExternalRefs.tsx`, shared by both
   panels. The backend sets the provider order (`_EXTERNAL_REF_PROVIDER_ORDER`
   in `api/dso.py`).
9. **Migrations.** Never edit an existing migration. Verify the upgrade on a
   copy of a database at 0065 and on a fresh one.

## 8. Left to the builder (research and decide in the plan)

- **PN G source:** settled by research as HASH (§11).
- **Fields and positions for each catalog,** in J2000. LDN gives an area,
  not a size. Any size derived from the area is an estimate: it must be
  stored and shown as one, or left missing.
- **The reviewed match lists** (§5.5):
  - Start each list from the matches its catalog states: 93 LBN, 7,553 MCG
    and 129 PN G alternate names in OpenNGC, and 233 LDN rows naming Barnard
    objects.
  - Add position candidates only after review.
- **Arp members missing from NightCrate.** Some members have no NGC or IC
  number, so they exist only once MCG is downloaded, or not at all. The plan
  proposes how Arp's own member positions are used. The aim is that a later
  MCG download merges through stated names rather than duplicating.
- **"Part of" schema and display:**
  - where it shows in the details panel and the alternate-names column;
  - how a search for the entry returns the members, and the whole when it
    exists.
- **Thumbnails and framing** for objects without a size.
- **Performance** with all catalogs loaded: the catalog page, facets, the
  planner and the load itself. Measure against today's timings.
- **Removal UI:** a confirmation that says how many objects go, and how many
  linked ones stay.

## 10. Process and verification

- **Process:** the full pipeline.
  1. An implementation plan.
  2. Codex plan discussion, then Fred's approval.
  3. Codex implementation, stage by stage.
  4. Independent reviews. The migration, the external data and catalog
     identity are high-stakes.
  5. Rendered checks, then Fred's acceptance test.
  6. The full suite once, at finalization.
- **Verification:**
  - **Upgrades:** at 0065 and fresh databases, with integrity and foreign-key
    checks.
  - **Add, remove and re-add each catalog** on a copy of a real database.
    Links keep their objects, no duplicates appear, and a removed catalog
    defers nothing.
  - **Live downloads through the mirrors,** with counts matching each
    ReadMe.
  - **Searches:**
    - LBN 437 and LBN 777 find their objects;
    - Mel 15 finds IC 1805;
    - Mel 25 finds the Hyades;
    - Arp 244 finds both galaxies, each marked as part of it;
    - Cygnus Loop finds the whole and its pieces;
    - PN G0.0+2.0 and PN G000.0+02.0 find the same nebula, while
      PN G000.0+02.0 and PN G000.0-02.0 stay distinct.
  - **Planner:**
    - Tonight opens with both limits ticked at the Settings values, and the
      list matches today's default list;
    - unticking Min size shows WR 134;
    - picking a catalog leaves both boxes as they were.
  - **Timings** of the catalog page and the planner, with all catalogs
    loaded and with today's set.

## 11. Research findings (2026-11-02)

Full tables were downloaded from the Harvard and IDIA mirrors and counted.
The plan carries the column-level details. Findings that change the design:

**Planetary nebulae and Abell PNe.**
- PN source: **HASH (V/163)**, filtered to Galactic true, likely and
  possible PNe (3,973 of 11,760 rows). It has native J2000 positions, sizes
  and an explicit PN status. Acker's catalogue has 1,143.
- HASH's terms ask for a citation (Parker, Bojičić & Frew 2016) and the
  acknowledgement "This research has made use of the HASH PN database at
  hashpn.space".
- **Only the Harvard mirror serves V/163.** When Harvard is down, this one
  download fails with a clear message.
- **Abell planetary nebulae stay a separate choice** (revised after the plan
  review, to keep Fred's rule that each catalog is optional).
  - "Planetary nebulae" and "Abell planetary nebulae" share one HASH
    download.
  - Abell covers HASH's 80 `Abell n` names, typed by HASH's current class:
    Abell 35 is ionised interstellar gas, and four are galaxies.
  - Five more Abell numbers come from a reviewed list: Abell 50 is
    NGC 6742.
  - The file stays while either catalog is on.

**Many-to-one cases.**
- **LDN** rows name up to eight Barnard objects each. **Claude's call:**
  - one Barnard object of comparable size is a stated match, so it is
    merged;
  - several Barnard objects, or one much smaller than the cloud, become
    parts of that LDN cloud (§5.1).
- **Arp (VII/192)** is Webb's 1996 index. It lists 592 member galaxies under
  338 numbers, positions each member, and gives no size for a group. That
  confirms §5.2: members only.

**Magnitudes and sizes.**
- **ACO** has no size. Its `m10` is the magnitude of the tenth-brightest
  member, not the cluster's, so magnitude stays missing and `m10` goes in
  the notes. **Wolf-Rayet** `vmag` is narrow-band v: it also goes in the
  notes, not `mag_v`.
- **Melotte sizes (Claude's call):** the new clusters take their
  positions from Hunt & Reffert 2023 where it has them (Mel 20, 66 and
  186). Their size is stored as twice the half-member radius `r50`,
  labelled as such in the list's notes. The Hyades and Coma keep OpenNGC's
  sizes.

**Data problems to handle.**
- LBN and LDN rows at −00° declination have wrong Galactic coordinates. The
  equatorial positions are right, as SIMBAD confirms (IC 423 = LBN 913 at
  −00°38′ B1950). Use the equatorial positions.
- MCG VII/62A reuses 5 identifiers for different positions, and its sizes
  are in 0.1′ units. VII/100 sizes are in arcmin.
- LBN and LDN positions are B1950, to the nearest minute of RA and
  arcminute of declination.

**Other.**
- SIMBAD's Harvard mirror answers identifier queries ("Mel 15" → IC 1805),
  so it can check the Melotte list.
- VizieR's terms are "free of usage in a scientific context" with the
  authors cited. They are not an open licence. Each user still downloads
  at runtime (§7.7).
