# v0.51.0 — Cleanup before Distribution

Fred started this version on 2026-11-12, after merging v0.50.0, and chose a
short version of promised and approved work ahead of Distribution (now
v0.52.0). The scope is in `PLAN.md` (v0.51.0 › Scope). Fred decided in
conversation on 2026-11-12:
- the slideshow's behaviour;
- the "Not recorded" label;
- after the design review, two more things:
  - the settings fixes (section 5);
  - keeping the first filter fix to batches that know their equipment
    filter.

Claude made the remaining choices; they are listed at the end for his
review. A clickable mockup of the slideshow and the swatches is at
`.superpowers/mockups/v0.51.0/slideshow.html` (local).

## Why

- **The slideshow** was promised in v0.46.0. The Gallery's full-size viewer
  brought the arrows forward and left "only automatic playing" for the next
  version, which never came. Fred wants to run it on a computer, a tablet and
  a phone.
- **The filter colour fixes** were promised on 2026-11-08 ("fix 1-3 in the
  next version"). They were recorded only in Fred's notes, so v0.48.0–v0.50.0
  missed them.
- **The Planner wording** and **the PixInsight path check** are small items
  approved for the deferred list.

## Goals

1. The full-size viewer can play a slideshow, from the Gallery and from the
   Messier page, on a computer, a tablet and a phone.
2. The slideshow speed starts at a default, can be changed, and is remembered
   the next time the app runs.
3. Filter swatches tell the truth for filters with no lines on record, for
   retired filters and for frames whose file names no filter.
4. The Planner's hints name its tabs as they are labelled.
5. A PixInsight project that names its image by a relative path gets a
   clear error, not a guess.
6. A bad saved setting resets only itself, and saving one setting never
   overwrites others with an older copy (added 2026-11-12, from the design
   review).

## Non-goals

- Making the Gallery or Messier pages comfortable on a phone (Fred: tapping a
  picture and running the slideshow is enough there).
- A slideshow button on the Gallery page, shuffle, or captions beyond what
  the viewer shows now.
- The two minor filter-swatch items left on 2026-11-08: 3+-line swatches
  drop the stripes of rare lines, and two makers' filters with the same model
  name could swap order.

## 1. The slideshow

### Fred's decisions (2026-11-12)

- A default speed; the user can change it, and the choice is remembered the
  next time the app runs.
- **↑ lengthens the interval and ↓ shortens it** (Fred, on reviewing the
  spec: "makes more intuitive sense"). His first wording had ↑ faster and
  ↓ slower.
- **← and → still step back and forward.** A step restarts the countdown for
  the new picture; it doesn't stop the slideshow.
- It must work on a tablet and a phone.
- On a phone, only the viewer needs to work well: the user taps a picture and
  runs the slideshow.
- It also works on the Messier page. That page already opens the same
  viewer, which steps through the tiles showing one of the user's own
  pictures, in Messier order; sky-survey tiles aren't in the viewer, and
  that stays.
- Accepted from Claude's proposal (Fred: "this sounds good"):
  - swipe left or right steps, like ← and →;
  - tapping the picture shows the controls; while playing they fade out
    after about 3 seconds; while paused they stay;
  - a control bar along the bottom: play/pause, the interval with its
    controls, and "3 of 12"; Close stays at the top right; touch targets are
    at least 44 px;
  - on a phone the side arrows are hidden and swiping replaces them;
    tablets and computers keep them;
  - the screen stays awake while playing, where the browser allows it;
  - one remembered speed for every device.

### Behaviour

**Starting and stopping.**
- The viewer opens paused, as today. Play starts the slideshow: the button,
  or Space.
- Space and the play/pause button toggle. Esc closes the viewer, as today.
- Closing the viewer stops the slideshow. The viewer already closes when its
  page hides (a persistent route), so a hidden page never plays.
- When the browser tab is hidden (another tab, the phone locked), the
  slideshow pauses and stays paused when the tab returns, with the controls
  showing so one tap resumes.

**Timing.**
- The speed is an interval in seconds per picture, from these steps: 2, 3,
  4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30. The default is 5.
- ↑ moves one step longer (more seconds), ↓ one step shorter. They stop at
  the ends. They also work while paused, so the interval can be set before
  pressing play.
- A change shows a short note in the middle of the screen, such as "8 s per
  picture" ("2 s per picture (shortest)" at an end), and updates the bar.
  The current countdown restarts at the new interval.
- **The countdown starts when the picture appears**, not when the step
  happens. A large full-size picture can take more than a second to load;
  starting the clock at the step would cut it short or skip it.
- A picture that fails to load shows the viewer's existing message, and the
  countdown still runs, so the slideshow doesn't stall.
- After the last picture the slideshow goes to the first and keeps playing.
  While playing, Next on the last picture goes to the first and Previous on
  the first goes to the last. While paused, the ends behave as today (the
  arrow is disabled).
- ← → and swipes restart the countdown for the new picture and keep playing.

**Remembering the speed.**
- One app setting, `gallery_slideshow_seconds`, added to
  `core/config.py:Settings` like `gallery_view`; no migration. The allowed
  values are the steps above. A stored value outside them falls back to 5,
  alone (section 5).
- Every speed change saves it, sending only that setting (section 5).
  Saving fails quietly (the speed still applies for this viewing); the
  existing settings store handles errors as it does for the Gallery's view
  choice.

**Controls and their visibility.**
- **The control bar** sits at the bottom, centred. It has no previous or
  next buttons; the side arrows and swipes do that. From left to right:
  play/pause; −; the interval ("5 s"); +; then "3 of 12" at the right end.
  − shortens and + lengthens the interval, like ↓ and ↑. The counter moves
  into the bar from its corner.
- **Layout by size** (mockup: `.superpowers/mockups/v0.51.0/slideshow.html`,
  local):
  - 900 px wide and more: the title and subtitle at the left, as today, with
    the bar centred on the same line;
  - narrower: the title centred above the bar;
  - phone width: the bar spans the width;
  - under 500 px tall (a phone on its side): one line under the picture,
    the title at the left and the bar at the right, with smaller padding.
  These follow the viewer's own size (the mockup uses container queries).
- **While paused** everything shows, as today plus the bar.
- **While playing** the bar, the side arrows, Close, the title and the
  counter fade out after about 3 seconds without input, leaving only the
  picture. They come back on mouse movement, a tap on the picture, or a key
  press, and stay while the pointer is over a control or a control has
  keyboard focus.
- A tap on the picture while controls show hides them again (playing only).
  A tap never steps or pauses; swipes step; the play button pauses.
- **Phone width** (below MUI's `sm`, 600 px): the side arrows are hidden, the
  viewer's 88 px side padding shrinks so the picture uses the width, and the
  bar stays reachable with a thumb. Landscape and portrait both work; the
  picture keeps its proportions.
- **Swipes** work on any touch screen, tablets included: a mostly-horizontal
  swipe of at least about 50 px steps; a short or vertical one does nothing.
  The viewer has no zoom, so the swipe doesn't fight a pinch. The app's
  viewport and gesture rules (development decisions, Tablet and LAN
  behaviour) stay as they are.

**Moving between pictures.**
- The new picture fades in over about 300 ms. With the system's reduced
  motion setting, it appears without a fade.
- The viewer already loads the pictures either side of the current one in
  advance; that stays.
- Both picture kinds count: a project's own image and a plate-solve picture
  with its marks (object search results). The countdown starts when either
  kind appears.

**Keeping the screen awake.**
- While playing, the viewer asks the browser to keep the screen on, and
  releases the request on pause, close or a hidden tab. Browsers allow this
  only over HTTPS (assumed from the browser documentation, to be checked on
  Fred's iPad over `make dev-lan`, which serves HTTPS). Where it isn't
  allowed, nothing is shown and the device's normal sleep applies.

**Keyboard and accessibility.**
- Keys are handled in the viewer's own `onKeyDown`, as today: no
  window-level listeners (development decisions, Gallery: the page is
  persistent). ↑ ↓ and Space are taken from the browser (no scrolling, no
  button activation twice).
- The play/pause button's label says what it will do ("Play slideshow",
  "Pause slideshow"); − and + have labels with the resulting interval
  ("Longer interval: 6 seconds per picture").
- "3 of 12" stops announcing to screen readers while playing (it would
  speak every few seconds) and announces again when paused.

## 2. Filter colours

Fred's three fixes of 2026-11-08, from the post-publication review of
v0.47.0, checked against the code on 2026-11-12. Filter colours live in
`components/projects/filterColors.ts` (development decisions, Projects:
Filter colours).

1. **Filters with no lines on record.** 17 of the 80 seeded filters have no
   passbands: 10 light-pollution, 3 UV/IR cut, 3 other, 1 neutral density.
   `resolveFilter` tries passbands, then the line name, then the label, and
   never the filter's type, so these show the grey "unknown" outline.
   - Resolve from the equipment filter's type when it has no passbands:
     light-pollution, UV/IR cut and neutral density take the striped
     whole-spectrum swatch (they pass the visible spectrum, filtered or
     dimmed), as the development decisions already say for UV/IR cut and
     light-pollution filters.
   - "Other" keeps the grey outline. The three seeded "other" filters are
     an IR-pass filter and two opaque dark positions in smart-scope filter
     changers; none is whole-spectrum.
   - User-made filters follow the same rule.
   - A bandpass or header named "ND" follows the equipment rule: both
     `canonicalLine` (bandpass names) and `lineFromName` (header names) map
     it to the whole-spectrum swatch instead of grey.
   - **Reach.** Only hand-entered batches are linked to an equipment filter
     (`filter_id`). Batches built from frames carry the header's name
     (`filter_label`) and no equipment filter, and the Files tab and the
     Overview look filters up by name. On Fred's database every batch is
     built from frames, so this fix changes nothing visible there.
   - **Fred's decision (2026-11-12): keep it as written.** Header names keep
     the colour their name implies. Working out which equipment filter a
     header name means would be automatic equipment inference from
     headers, which the settled scope removed. A per-rig link the user sets
     ("Duo-Band" is this filter) is a possible later feature.
2. **Retired filters on the Sessions tab.** It loads active filters only
   (`fetchFilters(false, false)` in `ProjectSessionsTab.tsx`), so a batch shot
   with a retired dual-band filter loses its split colour and sorts last.
   Load retired filters too for display; pickers that choose a filter stay
   active-only.
3. **Frames whose file names no filter** (no FILTER header). Today
   (checked 2026-11-12):
   - a batch built from such frames has `line_name = 'other'` and no
     `filter_label`, so the Sessions tab shows a grey "other";
   - the Files tab's Filter column is empty, and its filter pill reads "—".
     That pill can't filter: no pill selected and "no filter name" are both
     `null`, so it always looks selected and never narrows the list;
   - a batch entered by hand with the bandpass "Other" stores the same
     values, and the integration summary (`_integration_label` in
     `api/project_sessions.py`) puts both under one "other" bar.

   **Fred's decision (2026-11-12):** frames whose file names no filter show
   the grey outlined swatch with **"Not recorded"**. A missing header isn't
   proof there was no filter, so it stays unknown, distinct from "No filter".
   - "Not recorded" applies to batches built from frames (`source =
     'auto'`) with `line_name = 'other'` and no label. A hand-entered
     "Other" batch stays "Other": the user chose it.
   - The integration summary keeps the two apart, so the Overview bars and
     the exposure table, which both come from it, show "Not recorded"
     separately from "Other".
   - It sorts last, after unknown names.
   - The Files tab shows "Not recorded" in the Filter column, and its pill
     reads "Not recorded" and filters the list to frames with no filter
     name. That needs a query option on the frame list; today's broken "—"
     pill is fixed with it.

## 4. Relative paths in PixInsight projects

A PixInsight project can refer to an image file outside it by a stored
`filePath`. Four places open that path with `Path(...)` directly:
- `pxiproject_io.load_image_data` (`services/pxiproject_io.py`);
- the file-size lookup `_file_size_bytes` (`api/images.py`);
- the check when adding a project image, `_verify_project_image`
  (`api/projects.py`);
- the preview's file identity, `_pxiproject_identity`
  (`services/image_preview.py`; found by the plan review). Today a missing
  relative reference gives a generic preview 404 there.

A relative path resolves against the server's working directory, which
means nothing for the user's project. A project saved on Windows and opened
on a Mac or Linux has paths like `C:/…`, which also count as relative there.

- One shared check in `pxiproject_io` decides whether a stored path can be
  used, and all four places use it.
  - A Windows path on another system raises `PxiProjectError`: "This
    PixInsight project was saved on Windows and points to its image at
    C:/…, which this computer can't open."
  - Any other relative path raises `PxiProjectError`: "This PixInsight
    project gives the image's location as a relative path, so NightCrate
    can't find it."
  - An absolute path behaves as today: a missing file raises
    `FileNotFoundError`.
- The message must reach the user.
  - Today the image API turns only `ValueError` into a readable 422, so a
    `PxiProjectError` there becomes "Internal processing error" (500).
    Those endpoints translate `PxiProjectError` the same way as
    `ValueError`, so the Image Analyzer shows the message.
  - The file-size lookup treats an unusable path like a missing file: no
    size shown.
  - Adding a project image refuses it with the message. A missing
    referenced file there escapes as a server error today; it gets a clear
    refusal too.
- The preview reports an unusable path as an unreadable preview with the
  message. Plate solving and aberration already handle `PxiProjectError`;
  they don't change.

## 5. Settings: saving and loading

Found by the design review and checked in the code on 2026-11-12. Both
problems predate this version and affect every setting; the slideshow
speed makes them visible. **Fred decided (2026-11-12) to fix both in this
version.**
- **One bad value resets every setting.** `get_settings`
  (`core/config.py`) builds `Settings(**data)` and, on any
  `ValidationError`, returns `Settings()`: every field at its default. The
  next save writes the whole object (`update_settings` upserts every
  field), so the user's real values are overwritten for good. A later
  version that narrows a field's allowed values would trigger it.
  - **Fix:** an invalid stored field falls back to its default alone; every
    other field keeps its stored value. If the cross-field scoring check
    (`_validate_scoring`) fails, only the fields it covers fall back.
    Nothing is written while loading.
- **Every save sends every setting.** The settings store
  (`stores/settingsStore.ts`) merges the change into its copy and PUTs the
  whole object. A page loaded earlier on another device writes its old
  values back the next time it saves anything: set 8 s on the iPad, change
  the Gallery view on the Mac, and the Mac's old 5 s comes back.
  - **Fix:** a save sends only the changed fields. The backend merges them
    into the settings the user sees, validates the result, writes only the
    changed rows and returns the full settings. One exception: editing a
    threshold while the saved three are damaged saves all three as shown
    (call #21).
    - The backend does this in one transaction, so two saves at once can't
      break the scoring thresholds' order between them.
    - The store sends saves one at a time, in order, and keeps unsent local
      choices over each response.
    - The Planner sends only the fields it changed. (These three points come
      from the plan review.)
  - PUT with a whole object stays for the API page and older clients.
- These touch settings persistence and the API contract, so they get a
  fresh independent review.
